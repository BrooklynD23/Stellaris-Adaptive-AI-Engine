# Build a strategic director, not a smarter admiral

An adaptive, open-source AI engine for modded Stellaris is buildable today. In practice it is a **companion Python process that reads the game through log lines and autosaves and writes back through a console `run` file**. It infers your playstyle with a small Bayesian filter and retunes the AI empires' **economy, fleet doctrine and posture**. It does this through script variables and flags that an additive mod exposes. It never touches the vanilla difficulty bonuses. Nothing in the frontier research changes that shape. SIMA 2 and NVIDIA ACE work on pixels and dialogue, not symbolic strategy. The most relevant 4X benchmark, CivBench, had frontier LLMs win **3 of 23 Civilization VI games**. The evidence that holds up points to a deterministic engine that does its own monitoring, with authored counter-strategies and legible, slow-paced switches. The honest limit, which the advisor calls the **influence ceiling**, decides what the product can promise. The engine can change **when** the AI fights and **with what**. It cannot change **where** or **how**, because fleet pathing, targeting, engagement and war-declaration timing are hardcoded. Before any engine code is written, a one-weekend feasibility spike has to show four things. A script-emitted line reaches Python within 5 seconds. A `.ini` run file changes a variable on a chosen AI and is acknowledged. One knob produces a visible A/B difference over 15 game years. A 4.4 autosave parses in under 3 seconds. StarNet is a weaker foundation than it looks. Its GitHub tree targets 3.10/3.11, it predates the 4.0 economy rework, and it has no license. That makes choosing the baseline the first real decision.

*Provenance: Haiku 5.5 did the five research notes, Opus 5.5 wrote the three plans (integration, engine core, dev environment), and Fable 5.1 wrote an independent red-team review. Where the advisor's choices, accuracy flags, cuts or risk ranking conflict with the plans, this report follows the advisor and says so. The newest Stellaris release verified in the notes is **4.4.4 (June 24, 2026)** ([patched.gg](https://patched.gg/games/stellaris/stellaris-444-patch-released-5505)). Anything newer is unverified.*

## Frontier agents supply design patterns, not a drop-in brain

The headline systems you named are the least transferable. **SIMA 2** (Nov 2025) is a Gemini-based agent that reasons about instructions. It improves through a loop where Gemini proposes tasks and scores episodes, which then feed a self-generated experience bank. But its inputs are **pixels and keyboard/mouse**. DeepMind gives no success rates in the text, and it names long-horizon tasks and short memory as open limitations ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/sima-2-an-agent-that-plays-reasons-and-learns-with-you-in-virtual-3d-worlds/)). Nothing shows that loop working on symbolic state. The only idea worth taking for Stellaris is "a model proposes objectives and scores outcomes from snapshots", and the Claude-in-the-dev-loop role below already covers it. **NVIDIA ACE's adaptive boss, Asterion in MIR5**, was shown at CES 2025 and is described as "an AI boss that adapts to player tactics". However, no source explains what it tracks, how it learns, or whether it has shipped ([NVIDIA ACE for Games](https://developer.nvidia.com/ace-for-games); [InvenGlobal](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/19159/wemade-next-ceo-jeong-soo-park-shares-insights-on-mir5s-revolutionary-ai-boss)). The documented ACE components are speech, dialogue, animation and on-device small language models. The Nemotron-4 4B model targets role-play and function calling on RTX GPUs ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-decoded-gamescom-ace-nemotron-instruct)). Treat Asterion as motivation, not method.

The LLM-in-strategy-games literature is more useful because it is mostly **negative and specific**. In **CivBench** (Sept 2026), frontier models drove Civilization VI through 76 MCP tools. They won **3 of 23 games** at a cost of **$31–229 and 2–8 hours per game**. The authors trace the failures to "under-monitoring" (state was available but never queried), a **reflection–action gap** (stated plans were not executed), and lag in pursuing delayed payoffs ([arXiv 2609.02459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02459)). **SwarmBrain** reached **76% wins against Hard StarCraft II AI**. It worked because an LLM set macro strategy while a rule-based layer handled execution, and its losses came from command-execution and economic errors ([arXiv 2401.17749](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.17749)). **Voyager's** library of verified, reusable skills is the cleanest pattern for packaging strategies ([arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291)). Read together, these push the design in one direction. The engine should own monitoring and execution. The strategic layer should choose among **authored, verified templates**. Commitments should be enforced by code, not left to a planner.

**Opponent modeling** gives the actual algorithm. Synnaeve and Bessière's Bayesian plan recognition for StarCraft learns from replays and infers from fog-of-war "seen" flags. It costs about **0.01 s per inference and about 3 MB** ([arXiv 1111.3735](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1111.3735)). That is the right scale for a CPU process running beside the game. **Bayesian Policy Reuse** and **BPR+** keep a belief over a library of opponent types, pick the best response, and detect switches between stationary policies ([arXiv 1809.04240](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1809.04240)). **Safe-exploitation** work bounds how far a counter may stray from equilibrium when the model could be wrong ([Ganzfried, arXiv 2201.04266](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04266)). Only the principle transfers, because full game-theoretic solving is infeasible for Stellaris. For fairness and feel, the clearest published reference is **Alien: Isolation**. Its Director knows where the player is, but the alien must search. Unlocks never trigger on player deaths, and the designers say it "never cheats" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)). The Hamlet DDA authors warn that reactive adjustments make enemies "seem erratic" ([Hamlet PDF](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/pubs/Hamlet.pdf)). Hence the engine's rules: slow commitments, telegraphed switches, and inputs limited to what a player could see.

On **frameworks**, the open stack is healthy but mostly premature for this project. Gymnasium 1.4.0 and PettingZoo 1.27.0 (MIT) are current ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/gymnasium/), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pettingzoo/)). So are Stable-Baselines3 2.9.0, pycma 4.5.0 and OpenSpiel 2.0.2 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/stable-baselines3/), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/cma/), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/open-spiel/)), and for local inference llama.cpp with JSON-schema-to-GBNF grammars ([llama.cpp grammars](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/grammars/README.md)). No open-source LLM or RL AI for Stellaris, EU4 or HOI4 turned up. The advisor's arithmetic explains why RL is the wrong first tool here. A 24-month commitment gives roughly **100 strategy decisions per 200-year campaign**, spread across many arms. That is too few samples for online RL, for a within-campaign bandit, or for offline RL. The RL and game-theory libraries become relevant later, for tuning template parameters offline once a benchmark harness exists, and for games with fast simulators (see the last section).

## The influence ceiling: what the engine can and cannot move

The plans never stated this boundary, and it decides the pitch. The mod can only change things the game exposes as data or script state. Everything else stays with the hardcoded AI.

| Layer | What it covers | Live? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scriptable live** | `ai_budget` spending shares (via additive entries gated by `sae_*` flags), economic subplan selection (flag-gated), ship-**size** mix, starbase-defense toggles, opinion modifiers toward a target, policies (AI may revert), StarNet-style rally/defend nudges via timed flags | Yes, via `run` file and monthly event | Gating patterns **verified in StarNet's code** ([GitHub](https://github.com/OldEnt/stellaris-starnet-ai)); *efficacy unverified* |
| **Between-session only** | `NAI`/`NShip` defines (war-target priorities, designer ratios), `@scripted_variables`, default flag sets applied on game start | No, needs restart | Defines are data loaded at startup (verified in StarNet's `str_defines.txt`); works in Ironman |
| **Out of reach** | Fleet pathing and target selection, engagement logic, war-declaration timing loop, ship design beyond size mix (auto-designer), crisis and fallen-empire behaviour | — | Code-driven per the research notes; consistent with the absence of setters on the wiki Effects page ([wiki](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Effects)) |

Against a chokepoint turtle, the engine can make the AI **bigger, earlier, and armed with longer-range ships**. It cannot make the AI route around the chokepoint or siege it intelligently. The honest mod-page pitch, in the advisor's words: *"a strategic director that reshapes the AI's economy, fleet doctrine and posture in response to how you play"*, **not** *"an AI that outmanoeuvres you."* Two plan items fall below the ceiling, and the advisor cuts them. The engine-core plan's **role-based fleet composition** (artillery/torpedo/carrier) is cut because roles live in the auto-designer and in load-time defines. Only the per-size variable is a verified live knob. **`PRIORITIZE_TECH`** is deferred because no live `ai_weight` hook for research has been verified.

"Without vanilla bonuses" holds by construction. Every lever above reallocates what the AI already has. None of them adds resources, and the validator rejects any intent that would. One caveat: whether these reallocations move AI strength *enough to matter* is the **single highest-risk assumption** in the project. If the knobs are weak, there is no product, which is why M0 tests efficacy before anything else.

## The mod and bridge: four pipes, one of them pressed by the player

The integration design carries over from the plan with the advisor's corrections. The names are unified as **`sae`** everywhere: mod ids, the log prefix `SAE|v1|`, and the Python package. The dev plan's `sai` naming is dropped. The full loop:

```
┌──────────────── Stellaris 4.4.x (single-player, non-Ironman for live mode) ─────────────────┐
│  AI empires: vanilla 4.4 AI  — or a StarNet 4.x fork IF M0-1 finds a maintained, licensed one │
│  sae_bridge mod (additive only, every file/id prefixed sae_):                                 │
│    on_monthly_pulse_country ─► sae.1  emit SNAP/PLYR lines (managed AIs + player only)         │
│    on_monthly_pulse_country ─► sae.2  if seq_pending > seq_applied: validate, apply knobs,     │
│                                       set seq_applied, emit ACK, fire flavour event on switch  │
│    knobs: sae_* flags ► additive ai_budget / subplan entries; ship-size vars; opinion mods     │
└────┬──────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────▲──────────────┘
     │ log = "SAE|v1|…"  (H: path,  │ autosave_*.sav (monthly in dev)           │ player presses hotkey:
     │  flush ≤5 s)                 │                                           │ console › run sae_in.ini
     ▼                              ▼                                           │
 logs/game.log  ──tail──►   save games/<empire>/ ──jomini parse (H: ≤3 s)──►    │ Documents/Paradox Interactive/
     │                              │                                           │   Stellaris/sae_in.ini (atomic write)
┌────▼──────────────────────────────▼───────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────┐
│  sae companion process (Python ≥3.11, CPU-only, out of the game process)                      │
│   LiveAdapter (async): observations() · emit() → EmitReceipt · acks() · clock()               │
│     └ fog mask v0 = "diplomacy-screen legal" approximation, computed from the save            │
│   Engine core: features ► forgetful Bayes (3 archetypes) ► counter-template table ►          │
│     commitment (24 mo) + safety-floor rule ► Intent ► validator ► compiler ─────────────────┘ │
│   Bus: runs/<id>/bus.jsonl  (pydantic envelope; single source of truth; acks are events)      │
└────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
     ▼
 Rerun viewer (live + scrub)   ·   DuckDB over bus   ·   ReplayAdapter / SynthAdapter (offline dev, tests)
```

**Packaging and loading.** The mod is an ordinary folder with a `descriptor.mod` (`name`, `version`, `supported_version="v4.4.*"`, `tags`, `picture="thumbnail.png"`). It lives in the user mod directory: `Documents\Paradox Interactive\Stellaris\mod` on Windows, `~/.local/share/Paradox Interactive/Stellaris/mod` on Linux. It is enabled in the Paradox launcher or Irony Mod Manager like any Workshop mod ([wiki Modding](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Modding)). Load order is ASCIIbetical by filename, and override behaviour depends on the folder (FIOS/LIOS). The design is therefore **additive only**: new budget entries, scripted effects, script values and on_action hooks. The one unavoidable override file is named `zz_sae_*`. If StarNet is present, SAE loads after it and drives StarNet's own variables instead of overriding StarNet's files. The layout is `common/on_actions/sae_on_actions.txt`, `common/scripted_effects/`, `common/script_values/`, `common/ai_budget/sae_budgets.txt`, `events/sae_bridge_events.txt` and `localisation/english/sae_l_english.yml`. The advisor picked this event filename over the dev plan's `sai_telemetry.txt`. Changing `common/` alters the checksum and disables achievements, so SAE accepts that it is a gameplay mod.

**Data out.** The primary channel is a hidden monthly event that writes `log = "SAE|v1|…"` lines. The engine tails them from `game.log` by byte offset and handles truncation when the game launches. StarNet's code contains a `log` effect with localisation tokens (`log = "… [This.GetName] …"`). The output file, flush latency and survival in Ironman are **unverified**, and the effect is not on the wiki Effects page. The format is **pipe-delimited `key=value`**, not the dev plan's "json-ish" braces, because braces inside a Clausewitz string are a quoting risk. Countries are identified by **`[This.GetName]`, mapped to ids through the save**. The integration plan used `[This.GetID]`, which the advisor flags as unverified and doubtful for countries. Printing variables with `[This.var]` is documented on the wiki Variables page, which the advisor checked, and that lowers the risk of numeric emission. The **secondary channel** is the autosave. It is watched on disk and parsed with **jomini** (MIT; supports Stellaris; handles text, binary and zip envelopes) ([GitHub rakaly/jomini](https://github.com/rakaly/jomini)), or with stellaris-dashboard's MIT Rust parser. stellaris-dashboard is the proof that live save-watching works during play ([GitHub](https://github.com/eliasdoehne/stellaris-dashboard)). The log carries a small, high-frequency feature vector plus acks. The save carries ground truth and is used to reconcile the two. The integration plan also had a third channel, `run` a file containing `save sae_snap`. The advisor flags it as **probably impossible**, because the wiki lists no `save` console command. Use autosaves instead.

**Data in.** The live channel is the console **`run <file>`** command. It executes a list of console commands from a file in the user Stellaris folder ([wiki Console commands](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Console_commands)). The advisor's correction: the file is **`sae_in.ini`**, not `.txt`, because the wiki requires the INI extension. The engine writes it atomically (temp file plus rename). Each line is a fixed template, such as an `effect every_country = { limit = { has_country_flag = sae_managed_<name> } set_variable = { … } }`. The mod's monthly `sae.2` event then validates the ranges, applies the knobs, and emits an `ACK` line. **Who presses Enter** is the crux. The advisor recommends **hotkey-only in v0**: the player opens the console and types `run sae_in.ini`, which console history makes repeatable. Keystroke injection (AutoHotkey or xdotool) is fragile, depends on keyboard layout (the console key varies by layout ([wiki Console](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Console))), and can look like automation. It should become an opt-in feature later. If console `effect` cannot reach `every_country` scope, the fallback is `event sae.50 <target>` with the payload pre-staged in global variables.

**Modes.** The plans only modelled live play. The advisor adds a **release mode** as an adapter mode, with a `BetweenSessionEmitter` in the core. In release mode, observation is save-parse only (plus the log, if it survives Ironman). Influence comes from regenerating `scripted_variables/sae_generated.txt`, `zz_sae_generated_defines.txt` and the on-game-start flag set between sessions. This works in **Ironman**, because Ironman disables the console and with it `run`. The cost is per-session adaptation instead of live adaptation. **Multiplayer is out of scope.** A `run` on one client mutates local state and almost certainly causes an out-of-sync error, and patch 4.3.6 was still fixing OOS bugs tied to country resources ([patched.gg 4.3.6](https://patched.gg/games/stellaris/stellaris-436-patch-released-checksum-6ccb)).

**Who "self" is.** The engine-core plan modelled one AI against one opponent, and the advisor flags this as missing. In v0 the engine manages **only AI empires in contact with the player**, and they share **one belief about the player**. They are driven by per-country flags (`sae_managed_<name>`).

## The intent contract and engine core, cut to fit 100 decisions per campaign

The pipeline is planner → typed **Intent** (pydantic, `schema_version=1`) → **validator** → **compiler** → fixed template in `sae_in.ini` → mod applies → `ACK` → bus. The advisor keeps this unchanged as the best part of the plans. No planner, and no future LLM, ever writes raw console text. The reconciled vocabulary:

| Intent | Parameters (validated) | Compiles to | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SET_ECON_PLAN` | `country`, `plan` ∈ {**balanced, military, research, expansion**} | `sae_k_econ` enum → one `sae_econ_*` flag gating additive budget/subplan entries | Flag-gating verified (StarNet); efficacy **M0** |
| `SET_MILITARY_POSTURE` | `posture` ∈ {**defensive, deterrent, offensive**}, `target_id` | `sae_k_mil` → budget weights; opinion modifier toward target | Opinion-modifier effect **unverified** |
| `FLEET_COMPOSITION_BIAS` | per **ship size**, 0–100 | `str_var_ai_ship_data_factor_<size>` (StarNet) or SAE's own script values (vanilla) | Verified in StarNet 3.x code; 4.4 **M0-1** |
| `DIPLO_STANCE` | `target_id`, `stance` ∈ {friendly, neutral, rival, hostile} | `sae_dip_*` flag + opinion modifier | Weight hooks **unverified** |
| `PRIORITIZE_TECH` | `category`, `weight` 0.5–2 | — | **Deferred**: no live research hook verified |
| `NOOP_HEARTBEAT` | none | advances `seq` | Liveness test for the whole bridge |

These choices follow the advisor over the plans. The economic enum drops the integration plan's `alloys_rush`/`tech_rush`/`consumer_stability` set, and `consumer_stability` is cut as "not a counter-strategy". Posture takes the core plan's three values with a required `target_id`, and the integration plan's `intensity` scalar is dropped until some knob is shown to scale with it. Fleet bias is per size, not per role. Every batch carries a monotonic `seq` and `ttl_months`. The mod ignores stale and duplicate sequence numbers and logs `SCHEMA_MISMATCH` on a version skew. **Acks are first-class bus events.** An intent becomes `APPLIED` only when its matching `ACK` line arrives. After **K = 2 months** without an ack it becomes `LOST`, is re-emitted once, and then raises an alarm. The integration plan had a 6-month posture cooldown. The advisor deletes it because the 24-month commitment already covers it. Legibility has to travel through the same pipe, since the engine cannot push free text into the game. Each batch therefore sets a `reason_key` enum that selects a **pre-authored localisation string** for a flavour event such as "Detected fortified chokepoints; shifting to siege doctrine".

**Engine core, v0.** The core plan had 7 archetypes × 3 phases × 9 features, tempered naive Bayes, CUSUM, Normal-Inverse-Gamma contextual Thompson sampling with an LCB floor, CMA-ES, PSRO and a four-gate promotion pipeline. The advisor cuts this to what the sample size can support. The archetypes are **turtle, aggressor and builder**. The model uses **four or five save-derived features**: fortified border starbases relative to frontier length, expansion rate, the visible fleet-power ratio, months to first war or claim, and a tech proxy. It is a **plain forgetful Bayes filter** with fixed-share forgetting at a fixed ρ:

```
prior_t(a) = (1 − ρ)·b_{t−1}(a) + ρ/K
b_t(a)    ∝ prior_t(a) · Π_{f observed} clip(p(x_f | a, phase), ±L)
```

Unobserved features drop out of the product, which mirrors Synnaeve and Bessière's fog-of-war "seen" flags. The likelihood is clipped so that one newly revealed doomstack cannot snap the belief to a single archetype. Selection is a **lookup table** from the top archetype, when confident, to a counter-template, wrapped in a **24-month commitment**. The advisor's reasoning: about 100 pulls per campaign across 10–20 arms and a 15-dimensional context means a within-campaign bandit would never move off its prior. In v0 the counter table maps turtle → *outscale/siege doctrine* (military econ, larger and longer-range hull mix, offensive posture with a target). Aggressor maps to *hold and counterpunch* (defensive posture, starbase defenses, military econ). Builder maps to *early punish* (offensive posture against the player before its economy compounds). Below a belief threshold, the AI stays at baseline. The **safety floor** is a rule ("never below baseline budget share X"), not an LCB computation. The **reward window equals the commitment window**. Several plan items are gone from v0: CUSUM, τ fitting, the per-player Dirichlet profile, CMA-ES, PSRO and the ≤ 50 ms p99 tick target. The tick target goes because the game tick lasts seconds and the engine runs out of process. `fleet_conc` and `fleet_near_border` are **deferred**. Fleet sightings are not cheaply scriptable, and the save gives god-view positions, so those features need the fog-mask decision first. The tick loop keeps the plan's anti-CivBench structure. The engine checks alarms every month instead of waiting for a planner to ask, and the audit records every proposed-but-unemitted intent, which measures the plan/action gap directly.

**Fairness in v0** uses a "**diplomacy-screen legal**" approximation computed from the save: relative power categories, visible borders, and observed starbases in bordering systems. The strict per-(observer AI, target) intel mask is the right principle, but the advisor estimates it roughly doubles the mod and parser work. Nobody in the plans owned the mask, and the `SNAP`/`PLYR` lines emit god-view data for everyone. The god-view fields therefore go to a separate `observation_god` stream that the engine never reads. The dev plan claims a unit test "enforces" this boundary. The advisor's correction is that such a test is a **convention, not a guarantee**.

**The LLM is not in v0 or v1.** The core plan's own hypothesis 3 predicts that the advisor does not improve strength. Its gate fires rarely. Its output is a capped bump to an arm the table would have picked anyway. SwarmBrain supports a planner/executor split, not a *rarely consulted* advisor. The LLM is revisited only at M4 as benchmark condition D. Claude earns its place **in the developer loop** instead. It generates and reviews Clausewitz script, `script_values`, `ai_budget` entries and the `.ini` compiler, and every effect name must be checked against the 4.4 wiki or StarNet, because models hallucinate Paradox syntax confidently. It writes jomini extraction code and fixtures. It analyses replays to find "when did the player change, which decisions mattered". It labels your own campaigns for calibration, in the way CivBench used Haiku to label commitments. It also writes the legible-reason strings. A future runtime "commentator" that writes flavour text from the audit log is harmless, provided it stays off the decision path.

## Watching every decision live: the bus, Rerun and the Engine Console

The dev environment is built on one idea from the plan, which the advisor endorses: **one append-only event bus** (`runs/<id>/bus.jsonl`, a pydantic envelope with `v`, `run_id`, `seq`, `t_wall`, `game_date`, `type`, `decision_id`, `fow`, `payload`). The bus is the single source of truth, the replay format and the audit store at once. Event types are `observation`, `belief_update` (prior, likelihood, posterior, top evidence by log-likelihood ratio), `strategy_selected`, `intent_emitted`, `intent_ack`, `outcome`, `control` and `health`, with `observation_god` kept in a separate file. The engine-core plan's `DecisionAuditRecord` becomes the schema of a bus event type, not a separate writer. The decision audit is a **DuckDB view** joining rows on `decision_id`. SQLite comes back only for cross-campaign profiles at M4+. The **ReplayAdapter** plays recorded sessions back. It supports speed control, start and stop dates, and simulated ack models (echo, drop with probability *p*, delay), so the intent pipeline can be built with the game closed. The **SynthAdapter** generates archetype-labelled observation streams for unit tests. Both use the dev plan's **async adapter protocol** (`observations()`, `emit() → EmitReceipt`, `acks()`, `clock()`), which the advisor prefers to the core plan's synchronous version because it can drive replay and synthetic runs.

The advisor cuts most of the planned viewer stack: TensorBoard (duplicates Rerun scalars), Phoenix and OpenTelemetry (no LLM in v0), Parquet compaction (a 200-year campaign is tens of MB of JSONL), Minari/d3rlpy, the Windows CI leg, the FastAPI/Preact SPA, and every control command except `pause` and `force_strategy`. The v0 viewer is **Rerun**. It is Apache-2.0/MIT, its Python SDK bundles the viewer, and it supports named timelines (`game_date`, `tick`), time-series scrubbing and `.rrd` saves for replay. It needs **zero frontend code** ([GitHub rerun-io/rerun](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun)). A Rerun blueprint fixes the panel layout below. The SPA is built only once Rerun actually gets in the way. The wireframe is the dev plan's console updated to the reconciled vocabulary: three archetypes, a rule table instead of bandit arms, size-based fleet intents, `sae` naming, and no LLM panel in v0.

Two facts make unattended benchmarking more practical than the dev plan assumed. The advisor confirmed **`game_speed <n>` and `game_paused` as real console commands**; the dev plan had marked them "not found". `observe` and `play <id>` are verified as well. Unpausing in observer mode hands the player's empire to the AI, which is exactly what AI-vs-AI runs need ([wiki Console](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Console)). Any console use disables achievements, so benchmarks should run on a separate profile. The dev plan estimated 30–90 minutes per 100-year run at maximum speed. The advisor calls that **optimistic**, because mid-game slows sharply. Use small galaxies with few empires. Stellaris's determinism is unknown, so outcomes have to be treated as distributions over paired runs from the same start save, never as single runs.

## Milestones M0–M4 and the weekend that decides everything

The advisor sizes the plans at about **three times too big** for a first playable. The reconciled roadmap ships a v0 with this scope: single-player, non-Ironman, vanilla 4.4 AI (unless M0-1 finds a working StarNet 4.x fork), a hotkey-driven `run`, save-parse plus the optional log channel, three archetypes, three or four knobs, the rule table, a 24-month commitment, an in-game flavour event on each switch, and a JSONL bus viewed in Rerun.

The integration plan's go/no-go rule still applies. If the log, `run` and save-parse tests pass, live dev mode is viable. If only save-parse passes, fall back to save-only observation with between-session control. If both knob tests fail, the levers are too weak for live adaptation. The advisor also ranked the load-bearing assumptions by risk × cost, which tells you where to look first if the spike goes badly. In order: **knob efficacy**; **StarNet existing for 4.4** (the 4.0 rework of pops, jobs and districts into zones almost certainly broke its job-based triggers); console `run`; keystroke injection; `log` flush; save-parse latency; whether scripted AI profiles can stand in for human archetypes; and whether fog-of-war features can be computed. The second-to-last assumption is **circular**. A detector fitted on a vanilla AI wearing a "turtle" flag and tested on the same AI says little about a human turtle. That is why M2 calibrates on campaigns you have played and labelled yourself.

## Generalizing beyond Stellaris is a property of the seams

The core plan keeps the engine core free of game imports. A game adapter supplies observations, feature extraction, the intent vocabulary, its legality rules and a baseline. That separation is what makes generalization possible, but it is a design intent, not something tested yet. The pieces that would carry over are the bus, the adapter protocol, the Replay and Synth adapters, the forgetful Bayes filter, the commitment and safety-floor logic, and the intent → validator → compiler pipeline. The pieces that would not carry over are every knob, every feature, and the influence ceiling, which has to be rediscovered for each game. Other Paradox titles are the natural next targets, because jomini already parses Paradox save formats ([GitHub](https://github.com/rakaly/jomini)). However, EU4 and HOI4 modding hooks were **not researched**. For 4X games with real APIs, CivBench drives Civilization VI over the FireTuner protocol (one connection only) ([arXiv 2609.02459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02459)). CivRealm wraps Freeciv in a Gymnasium interface under GPL-3.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/bigai-ai/civrealm)). The RL and game-theory stack from the frontier section fits those environments, with PettingZoo for multi-agent turns, Stable-Baselines3 for policies, OpenSpiel for PSRO over a payoff matrix, and pycma for template parameters. Those games can be stepped quickly, which Stellaris cannot. The sensible order is to finish M4 in Stellaris first, then write a second adapter for a game with a fast simulator before trying to make the core learn anything heavier than a lookup table.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The goal is not an AI that out-thinks you. Stellaris hardcodes the parts of play where out-thinking happens: pathing, targeting, engagement and war timing. The goal is an AI whose **strategy visibly answers yours**: it reads your turtle and arrives two years earlier with battleships and an announced siege doctrine. On that framing, the published evidence agrees across very different sources. The Alien: Isolation design notes, the Hamlet DDA paper, CivBench's failure analysis and SwarmBrain's split all show that adaptation feels smart when it is **legible, paced and fair**, and that the decision layer should be cheap and deterministic. The adaptive part is a Bayes filter that would fit on a floppy disk. The actual engineering work is in the plumbing and the proof.

The project lives or dies on one weekend experiment. Can a flagged budget entry or a ship-size variable make an AI empire measurably stronger in a way you can feel? If yes, the rest is two months of careful, mostly unglamorous engineering to a first playable that is honest about its ceiling. Only after paired benchmarks show that the simple version works do the bandit, the per-player profile, the LLM and other games earn a place. If the knobs prove weak, the same architecture drops back to between-session adaptation. That version is less magical, but it works in Ironman, where most players actually are.
